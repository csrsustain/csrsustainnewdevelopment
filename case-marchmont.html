Elevating energy performance across a 175-building portfolio

Industrial and office portfolio taken from EPC C–G toward a minimum B rating, well ahead of the MEES deadline.

Background

Marchmont, a property portfolio manager, partnered with CSR Sustain to improve energy performance across an extensive portfolio of industrial and office buildings. The objective was MEES compliance — elevating EPC ratings to a minimum of B well ahead of the deadline.

Client: Marchmont Investment Management  ·  Location: Nationwide

What we did

  • Electrification of heating — gas systems replaced with air-source heat pumps in office amenities and localised electric radiant panels in warehouses
  • Lighting upgrades — inefficient systems replaced with LED technology throughout
Scope & approach

How the programme ran

Portfolio benchmarking

Energy performance benchmarking across 175 buildings initially rated EPC C to G, giving a comprehensive view of efficiency status and highlighting priority assets.

MEES retrofit pathway advice

Bespoke retrofit pathways for 81 buildings, setting out strategies to reach a B rating and addressing both operational inefficiencies and portfolio-specific constraints.

Post-completion certification

Following implementation of upgrade works, 17 buildings were certified as achieving a B rating, meeting MEES compliance and Marchmont's sustainability objectives.

Challenges

What made this difficult

01

Industrial sites with high heating demand

Warehouses relied on gas convection heaters, with office amenities on gas boilers and radiators — both energy-intensive and inefficient.

02

Cost constraints on fabric upgrades

Insulation and glazing improvements were impractical: high capital cost, minimal impact on EPC rating.

03

Varying building characteristics

No two buildings were alike — differing ages, fabric, business activities and HVAC plant demanded a tailored, cost-effective route to B for each asset.

Results

The outcome

Calculated energy consumption reduction of 3,035,290 kWh (–72%) and carbon reduction of 586,301 kgCO₂ (–83%), with 17 buildings certified at B and further certifications planned as works complete.

What we learned

Lessons

Takeaways

  • Tailored solutions for industrial portfolios — electrifying heating and prioritising lighting proved critical
  • Value of early action — starting compliance planning early enabled incremental, cost-effective improvement
  • Focus on practicality — avoiding high-cost, low-return upgrades ensured efficient use of capital
